FAQ

Questions? We've got answers.

What is alkaline water?

Alkaline water has a higher pH than tap water or reverse osmosis water. Fans say it eases digestion, supports the immune system and helps the body recover after intense exercise. You can fill up on it at our U-Fill station.

What is hard water?

Hard water carries lots of dissolved minerals such as calcium and magnesium carbonate. It's fine to drink, but it builds scale in pipes, showerheads and appliances. It also makes every cleaning job harder: dingy laundry, spotted dishes, film on shower doors, and hair that feels sticky and dull.

Do I need a water softener?

Groundwater and well water are usually hard. Watch for scale on appliances, dry skin and hair, grey or faded clothes, stains on sinks and tubs, frequent plumbing repairs and rising water bills. Even on municipal water, a softener helps appliances run better and leaves skin, hair and linens nicer. Bring in a water sample for a free test, or try the 60-second check above.

How much does a water softener cost?

It depends on the type, size and complexity of the system. Visit us and we'll find the best match for your budget and your needs.

What are the benefits of soft water?

A softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium ions, then recharges itself with a salt solution. Soft water protects your appliances and plumbing. It also makes clothes cleaner, softer, brighter and longer-lasting, gives you spot-free glassware, and leaves skin and hair silky smooth.

Is softened water safe to drink?

Yes. A properly working softener adds only about 75–100 mg of sodium per quart. A slice of white bread has about 120 mg. If you'd like less, you can regenerate with potassium chloride, or add a reverse osmosis system for sodium-free drinking water.

What are the main operating costs?

Mainly softener salt, which we stock and deliver. The water and energy a softener uses to regenerate are smaller, secondary costs.

Will a softener increase my water bill?

Softeners use some water to regenerate. Most models we sell and recommend are energy- and water-efficient. Softeners can also save money: your dishwasher, water heater and washer last longer, and you need less detergent and water to get things clean.

Will a water softener harm my septic system?

A good-quality softener that's set up and maintained properly is fine for a septic system. Salt doesn't harm the bacteria or soil in the drainage field. Backwash runs about 55–100 L a week, so regenerating no more than once a week keeps it in check. Soft water also means you use fewer soaps and chemicals. A poorly running softener can over-regenerate, which is why proper setup matters.

Is softened water corrosive?

No. Ion-exchange softening doesn't change how corrosive water is. Corrosion comes from pH, dissolved oxygen, ammonia, chloride and flow velocity, and softening doesn't affect any of them.

Are water softeners noisy?

Hardly. The only sound is water moving through the unit during the backwash cycle.

When should I not use softened water?

Softened water isn't recommended for lawns and outdoor use, so every system we install includes a bypass valve. If you'd prefer no added sodium or potassium in your drinking water, we can add a separate cold-water line, or better yet, a purified drinking water system.

How do I maintain my softener, and where does it go?

Most softeners need little care. A properly sized unit should regenerate about once a week, which is usually enough for a family of four. Softeners go where the water line enters your home and should be installed by a professional. That's us.
